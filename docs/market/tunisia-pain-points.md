# Tunisia e-commerce pain points

Research notes on selling online in Tunisia. Every claim carries one label:

- [V] verified: checked against a primary or directly citable source.
- [VC] vendor claim: stated by a vendor or platform about itself or its product.
- [I] inferred: our reasoning from the facts above, not stated by a source.

Where a source is a company's own statement or press coverage, the source type is named in brackets.

## Competitors and platforms

- converty.shop homepage says free up to about $3,000 in sales. Its long text and FAQ say 10,000 TND. The two figures contradict each other and should not be used until resolved. [V] (contradiction)
- Shopify treats cash on delivery as a manual payment method. [V]
- About 860 Shopify stores operate in Tunisia. [VC]
- Jumia exited Algeria in early 2026. [V]
- TikTok Shop's 2026 expansion covers Europe only. [V] Tunisia is not in that expansion. [I]
- Paymee froze funds in 2023, per its own press release. [company statement]

## Regulation

- Tunisia's social-selling bill 42/2024 is in committee. [V, press]
- Ministry of Commerce seller disclosure rules were issued in June 2025. [V]
- A 30 June 2026 decree is reported in press but not confirmed by a primary source. Treat as unverified. [V, press] (reported, unconfirmed)

## Cash on delivery and refusals

- About 30% of COD orders are refused. This is one press figure from a single source. [V, single source]
- Phone confirmation before dispatch cuts refusals by 5 to 8 points. [VC]
- Impact for us: at a 30% refusal rate, a COD order that is not confirmed by phone is a likely loss, so confirmation flow matters more than checkout polish. [I]
